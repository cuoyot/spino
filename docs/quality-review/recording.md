## 2026-10-18 — Camera quality, framing and controls (D-061)

The owner reports poor quality and an unchangeable initial zoom. The previous center crop filled a fixed portrait/landscape frame and could discard much of a landscape sensor's field of view. The new compositor fits the full source at 1×, retaining black bars when aspect ratios differ. It now processes preview and saved frames identically, with a fresh canvas track at recording start to exclude buffered preview frames and retain the title/date caption from the first saved frame.

Capture requests 1920×1080, up to 30 fps, with ideal no-resize mode and no forced source aspect ratio. Output keeps the chosen start orientation at up to 1080×1920 or 1920×1080, bounded by the source's long edge. The target bitrate increases from 2 to 8 Mbps; the existing 250 MB/15 minute limits remain, so the size limit can end a high-detail take earlier. Source quality and actual bitrate remain device/codec-dependent.

Zoom resets inherited hardware magnification to 1× where supported, exposes the available range (including wider values if reported), and otherwise provides labeled 1–4× digital zoom. Fixed brightness adjustment spans 50–150%. Both remain available while recording, and update the same compositor without restarting the recorder. Changing cameras resets these local controls. Lock exposure requires manual and continuous modes and readable exposure time; it freezes current exposure time and ISO where available. Unlock restores continuous exposure. Applied settings are read back, and rejected/ignored controls remain recoverable. Without lock support, + Info explains that automatic exposure may still change; fixed image brightness does not pretend to lock the sensor. Camera APIs follow the [W3C Image Capture specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/image-capture/).

## 2026-10-18 — Sharing saved recordings (D-062)

The owner requests sharing immediately after saving and directly from Multimedia. Saved viewers and saved library cards now expose the standard connected-circles Share icon, with a localized accessible name. Successful Save keeps the saved viewer open and focuses Share; Close explicitly returns to the prior screen. Draft Save/Discard, retry on storage failure, title editing and deletion remain. Sharing does not remount the playback element. Concurrent share taps are suppressed, and cancellation is quiet. Failures report that the original is still saved and permit retry.

The shared payload is the original persisted Blob, with a filename derived from the original title/date and the correct audio/video MIME type. Filename separators are sanitized without changing notebook or recording titles. Android uses a dedicated MediaShare bridge: a private temporary cache copy is written sequentially in chunks of at most 256 KiB, with offset/size validation, then sent through the Android chooser using a FileProvider content URI and temporary read access. This avoids converting an entire 250 MB take to a single bridge payload. Incomplete staging is released on failure; successful share copies are retained for delayed receiver reads and cleaned after 24 hours on a later share. The original local file, notebook, PDFs and annotations are never rewritten for sharing.

[Twenty responsive frames](captures/multimedia/2026-10-18-share-04/verification.json) cover saved review and Multimedia in both themes, phone/tablet orientations and 320 px. Browser checks compare shared-file SHA-256 with the persisted recording, retain playback element identity, check immediate post-save access, prevent duplicate taps, and cover cancellation, error/retry and download fallback. Unit checks verify bounded chunk reconstruction, MIME/filename mapping and partial-copy cleanup. [Native emulator evidence](captures/multimedia/2026-10-18-share-native-05/native.json) records replacement installation, original notebook/blob retention, actual audio/video chooser foreground, temporary-file hashes and post-save sharing. Physical recipient-app acceptance remains open; no message or file is sent to a recipient during verification.

## 2026-10-18 — Expressive audio animations (D-063)

The owner selects **Silk waves**, **Orbit** and **Mixer**, requests one-finger changes in any direction and sets Silk waves as the default. The standalone audio recorder now devotes its available space to the visualization, with the recording status, small style selector and Stop and review action outside the canvas. Every new take starts with Silk waves. Right/down moves forward through Silk waves → Orbit → Mixer; left/up moves backward, wrapping. The style button provides a native keyboard/click alternative. Short taps, pointer cancellation and multi-finger gestures are ignored. Changing the visualization never replaces the recorder or recording stream.

Actual microphone RMS drives the wave/orbit amplitude and FFT bands drive the mixer. Motion settles during silence; no synthetic sound is used in production. Reduced-motion preference freezes traveling phase and samples visible input less frequently. Light/dark palettes preserve colored marks and legible labels. Canvas resolution is capped at 2× device pixel ratio; normal rendering is limited to approximately 30 fps. Stop/save/discard releases the microphone monitor, animation callback, observers and gesture listeners. Failure to initialize the monitor keeps audio recording/save available. Saved audio is the original stream, without a visualization track or schema changes.
