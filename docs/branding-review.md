## Historical preliminary research

Research date: 2026-09-30. The following records the initial public-source assessment before the owner's naming decision. It does not record a trademark registration or legal clearance.

| Candidate | Pronunciation and product fit | Preliminary assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Spino / SPino** | Short and reasonably easy to say. A Spanish speaker is likely to use “es-PI-no”; English readers may choose “SPEE-no” or “SPY-no,” so the intended pronunciation is not automatic. “Smart Piano Notebook” is an understandable expansion, although the letters are a coined contraction rather than a strict acronym. | Stronger of the two for recall and pronunciation, but existing app names mean it needs careful distinctiveness and trademark review. Title-case **Spino** is easier to read than internal capitals. These pronunciation judgements are editorial, not user-test findings. |
| **SPiwor** | The “wor” ending is ambiguous in Spanish and English, and the intended phrase is hard to recover from the spelling. **Workbook**, rather than **wordbook**, expresses a practice/exercise notebook in English. | Less convincing for speech, spelling and search. It also resembles the unaccented spelling of Polish **śpiwór**, meaning a sleeping bag, which introduces unrelated search traffic. The meaning is documented by the [Polish Academy dictionary](https://wsjp.pl/haslo/podglad/15328/spiwor). |

Google Play already lists [SpiñO(spino): Guide to Freedom](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.erezul77.spino), [Spino Barber](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.spinobarber.working) and a [Spino Studio developer](https://play.google.com/store/apps/dev?id=7587486758329177105). These are evidence of existing uses, not proof of a blocking registration or of permission to coexist. No conclusion about SPiwor availability follows from a sparse web search.

The primary name check should cover identical and similar marks, territory, earlier dates and relevant goods/services. Spain/EU are a sensible starting territory for this project, but distribution territories need confirming. Software and music/education services are relevant search categories; an adviser should determine the actual filing specification. Search variants such as Spino, SPino, Spiño, Espino, SPiwor and phonetically similar forms rather than only exact capitalization.

The [OEPM search page](https://oepm.es/es/herramientas/buscador-base-de-datos/buscador-marcas-y-nombres-comerciales/) provides Spanish-effects trademark tools and links to TMview/WIPO. The [EUIPO availability guide](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/en/trade-marks/before-applying/availability) explains similarity and goods/services checks. Even an empty search cannot guarantee freedom from challenges, as the [EUIPO FAQ](https://www.euipo.europa.eu/the-office/help-centre/tm/faq-search-availability) makes clear. TMview loaded only an interactive shell in the research tool; no reliable complete registry search/result set was obtained. That initial search did not establish clearance for either candidate.

Trademark and copyright are different issues. The proposed commercial name calls for trademark/confusion review; original logo artwork, code, text and musical editions have separate copyright questions. Do not repeat a blanket claim that names/titles can never have copyright protection: Spain's [Intellectual Property Act, article 10.2](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#a10) provides protection for an original work title as part of the work. That provision does not establish protection or infringement for either candidate here.

The initial recommendation favoured Spino over SPiwor. The owner subsequently chose the spelling **SPino** and the full name **SPino: Smart Piano Notebook**. No domains or handles were reserved or purchased during the repository work.
