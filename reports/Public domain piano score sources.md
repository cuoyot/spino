# Expand piano discovery through verified editions

SPino can expand beyond its current catalogue through **institutional historical PDF collections**, especially e-rara, the Library of Congress, Spain's BNE and Sibley/Eastman. The useful unit is an exact edition and downloadable file with recorded rights evidence, rather than a composer or a search result. This research identified concrete acquisition leads, but **only one PDF was successfully fetched during the research pass; binary validation is otherwise incomplete**. No new scores were added to the app. Public access, public-domain composition status and permission to redistribute the institution's digital reproduction are separate questions. A source's US or Australian public-domain assessment does not establish Spain/EU or worldwide clearance. Only validated exact PDF/XML files with rights evidence should enter the visible catalogue; the remaining exploration belongs in research documentation.

Research date: 21 October 2026. This report synthesizes the accompanying Europe, Americas and global research notes. Institutional rights statements below are reported source claims, not independent legal clearance.

## Prioritize institutions with explicit item rights

**e-rara offers the strongest immediately identifiable combination of old piano editions, item-level Public Domain Mark and PDF/IIIF access.** BNE is a particularly useful next discovery target for Spanish repertoire because its reproduction policy expressly addresses public and commercial reuse, subject to each item's conditions. Library of Congress collection-specific free-use statements and Sibley's classical repertoire make the next acquisition pair. These priorities reflect evidence quality and integration practicality, not a finding that every item in those institutions is reusable. ([e-rara interfaces](https://era-prod11.ethz.ch/wiki/apiinfo), [BNE reproduction policy](https://www.bne.es/es/servicios/reproduccion-documentos/uso-reproducciones), [LOC rights example](https://www.loc.gov/item/2023823592/), [Sibley collections](https://www.esm.rochester.edu/sibley/generalcollections/))

## Clear composition, edition and reproduction separately

**“Without copyright” should become an internal evidence requirement, not a blanket catalogue label.** A composition, arrangement, editorial contribution, cover artwork and digital reproduction can have different conditions. BNE explains Spain's usual seventy-year term and the transitional eighty-year term for authors who died before 7 December 1987. NLA likewise distinguishes musical-work rights and published-edition protection, and warns that catalogue assessments can depend on incomplete information. ([BNE author guidance](https://www.bne.es/es/servicios/autores-dominio-publico), [NLA music rights](https://www.library.gov.au/services/copyright-library-collections/rights-and-music-collection))

Accordingly, a US library's public-domain statement or Australian out-of-copyright flag must remain attributed to that source and jurisdiction. Ravel's *Sonatine* is a concrete example where successful file access and an institutional public-domain statement do not finish the territorial assessment. A PDM also records an institution's assessment rather than independently proving worldwide clearance. Preserve edition and rights evidence even where the composer is unquestionably historical; modern fingerings, introductions or arrangements cannot inherit a composer's status. ([Sibley exact item](https://urresearch.rochester.edu/institutionalPublicationPublicView.action?institutionalItemVersionId=19375), [BNE guidance](https://www.bne.es/es/servicios/autores-dominio-publico), [music contributor guidance](https://libraryguides.mcgill.ca/MusicCopyrightFAQ))

The proposed ingestion sequence is a small e-rara PDM pilot, followed by selected LOC pedagogical works and BNE exact-edition discovery; Sibley and NLA follow where contributors and territories can be resolved. Discovery records stay outside product assets. Acquisition records should retain the stable institutional ID, exact file URL, composer/arranger/editor identities, publication/imprint, source rights statement and URL, intended territories, attribution text and dated reviewer decision. Technical acceptance adds HTTP success, MIME/signature, SHA-256, page count or XML parsing, completeness and visual inspection on a phone. Only then should the file receive a stable catalogue ID and a preview generated from its cleared notation pages. This is a proposed process, not a claim that these checks have already passed.

## Preview first; publish difficulty only with provenance

**A first-notation-page preview, instrumentation and page count can help browsing without inventing a grade.** Generate previews only from cleared exact files; a cover alone often does not reveal the music. For unassessed entries, “Difficulty not assessed” is more truthful than assigning a level from titles or composer names. Publisher phrases such as “easy piano solo” can remain attributed descriptions, searchable but distinct from a reviewed rating.

Henle documents a nine-level scale, with levels 1–3 easy, 4–6 medium and 7–9 difficult; its judgments consider technical and musical demands. ABRSM grades belong to particular syllabus periods and repertoire/edition entries. Neither system is a universal conversion for the other, and a simplified arrangement must not inherit the original work's rating. No open bulk-reuse licence for either rating database was established here. Selected sourced references require matching the work, movement and arrangement and recording system, value, source URL, syllabus years where relevant, reviewer and verification date. ([Henle scale](https://www.henle.de/us/Levels-of-Difficulty/), [ABRSM syllabuses](https://www.abrsm.org/en-gb/syllabuses), [official 2025–2026 piano syllabus](https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20%26%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf))

No reviewed public archive supplied a clearly reusable standardized difficulty dataset. CIPI has expert-reviewed Henle-derived labels, but its score files require restricted academic access and non-profit/research use; it is **excluded as an unrestricted app source**. ASAP provides MusicXML but uses **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, so it too is excluded from an unrestricted redistribution pipeline. A derivative repository's licence headline cannot erase upstream restrictions. Automated predictions, if developed later from permitted inputs, must be labelled estimates and evaluated separately from published grades. ([CIPI access terms](https://zenodo.org/records/8037327), [ASAP licence](https://github.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset/blob/master/LICENSE.md), [derivative README](https://github.com/cperezs/piano-difficulty-dataset))

## Conclusion

A useful expansion milestone is a small set of fully inspectable, attributable scores with representative easy and intermediate repertoire, rather than a larger count of unresolved references. The next research effort should spend less time collecting repository names and more time resolving exact files, identifying contributors and reviewing their first musical pages. That yields catalogue records users can actually evaluate and download while keeping unfinished rights and editorial research out of the app.
