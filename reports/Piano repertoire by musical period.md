# Expand piano repertoire through identifiable editions

Research snapshot: 21 October 2026. These are research candidates, not catalogue additions or a release announcement.

**Albéniz’s original solo-piano Rumores de la Caleta is now included locally; prioritize further Spanish piano works, then broaden Classical and Impressionist coverage.** The initial research identified 49 specific work identities, of which Rumores is now locally included and 48 remain proposed: five Baroque, ten Classical, sixteen Romantic, and eighteen across French impressionism, related national schools and early modern repertoire. Their titles and catalogue numbers distinguish works from editions, movements and collections; final comparison against the live catalogue remains mandatory. The sixteen Romantic PDFs and the subsequently checked Albéniz Rumores PDF have full local byte/signature/hash evidence in this research; browser-parsed PDFs and repository metadata have weaker verification status. The current local implementation already reports **129 editions, 106 conservatively identified underlying works**, with five unresolved legacy identities excluded from that work count; that baseline is not a claim that every represented multi-movement work is complete. Its distribution—36 Baroque, 8 Classical, 51 Romantic, 3 Impressionist, 7 early modern and 1 unclassified—makes variety more valuable than another large Bach or study batch. This report proposes the next 14-work acquisition queue, identifies exact files and rights claims, and keeps every unresolved record outside the app. The local asset split is 55 XML/MXL and 74 PDF editions; this update has not been published to the website or native release.

The local baseline comes from the [canonical audit](../research_notes/Public%20domain%20piano%20score%20sources/canonical-audit-2026-10-21.json) and the coordinator's current implementation snapshot; it is separate from the external research below. Bach BWV 787–801, Satie Gymnopédies Nos. 2–3 and Debussy's second Arabesque are already included locally and are not proposed again. Existing first Arabesque, Clair de lune, Gymnopédie No. 1, Gnossienne No. 1, familiar legacy arrangements and the earlier Burgmüller/Schumann batches are likewise excluded from the new-work proposals.

## Albéniz original piano scores now take priority

**Rumores de la Caleta, Malagueña, Recuerdos de viaje Op.71 No.6** is the first completed local Spanish acquisition in this priority batch. David Surtees's exact Mutopia-2006/12/29-898 engraving explicitly identifies piano, collection and number, with a public-domain dedication; it is not a guitar transcription. The historical source is Unión Musical Española, year unspecified. Its complete eight-page PDF was fetched, parsed and rendered page by page, including the closing cadence and contributor credit. The PDF is **153,976 bytes**, SHA-256 `6b8eac839458eddb4e1dd463be2b518a48b8f725798fc31cd974a7ade0e16328`; the complete LilyPond source is 23,120 bytes. This adds file-level evidence to the earlier browser-only candidate, not a second work. ## Classical repertoire should follow the Spanish priority

The Classical queue supplies **ten proposed distinct works**, including complete-score candidates by Clementi, Kuhlau and Beethoven. A complete sonata counts once even when Mutopia supplies one PDF per movement. Haydn's two sonatas currently have only first-movement binaries inspected, so their remaining movements must be acquired before a complete-sonata listing. Mozart K.397 is deliberately held: the modern contributor's public-domain declaration does not settle the unusual 1951 historical source claim or editorial ending. Dussek's optional violin source must retain its instrumentation information rather than silently being advertised as an unequivocal unaccompanied original.

## Inclusion requires three rights layers and one work identity

**Composition status, editorial contribution and digital reproduction terms require separate evidence.** Mutopia's PD label describes the contributor's dedication; CC BY and CC BY-SA allow commercial reuse under their conditions. Preserve contributor names, exact licence version, source and revision, score footer credits, and an indication of extraction or other modifications. Share-alike obligations must be addressed for adapted score assets and previews; they should not be casually extended to unrelated app code. LilyPond `.ly` is not MusicXML. The Boulanger MXL files are genuine XML candidates at metadata level, but have not been retrieved or parsed. ([Mutopia legal policy](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/legal.html), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/), [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/))

A US institution's public-domain claim does not establish worldwide clearance. Spain's transitional duration rules and French wartime-extension provisions require territory-specific review, particularly for the later French repertoire; this research does not adjudicate the relevant calculations. **Ravel stays on hold even with a real fifteen-page PDF**, and Granados, Fauré and Boulanger additionally need the indicated exact binary/provenance checks. No NC edition is proposed for unrestricted redistribution. Gallica commercial reproduction terms, Austrian Google/ABO noncommercial restrictions and modern protected scholarly editions cannot be bypassed by choosing an old composer. ([Spanish transitional provision](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930#dtcuarta), [French L123-9](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006278951/2018-08-28), [BnF commercial reproduction](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/faire-une-utilisation-commerciale-dune-reproduction), [ABO policy](https://www.onb.ac.at/digitaler-lesesaal/austrian-books-online-abo))

Before any import, reconcile composer, catalogue/opus, numbered piece, instrumentation and collection relationship against the live canonical ledger. Record the final exact file URL, source item, source edition/editor/year, modern contributor, licence, intended territories, retrieval date, byte length and SHA-256. Validate a real PDF/XML rather than an HTML redirect; inspect all movements, first and last notation, representative middle pages, attribution and phone legibility. Generate the preview from that exact cleared asset. Retain existing record IDs and personal progress; attach another edition to its work instead of inflating the count. Research placeholders and unresolved rights notes stay outside the app. Difficulty remains unassessed until a traceable exact-version source or reviewed editorial assessment exists; neither proprietary ratings nor automatic title-based levels are imported.

The **49 initially researched identities** comprise 5 Baroque + 10 Classical + 16 Romantic + 18 adjacent French/Spanish/early modern pieces, with no repeated work identity apparent across the three researcher lists. With Rumores now included locally, 48 of those identities remain candidates. This is a research-pool count pending final live deduplication, not 49 guaranteed additions, not a complete set of 49 locally verified files, and not a projection of a new available-work total. Couperin's collection, unnamed Handel identities, Rameau's collection and unidentified e-rara Mozart sonatas are excluded. Count a complete sonata once; count independently numbered short pieces once each; never count both an anthology and its already counted members.

After the completed local inclusion of the verified **Albéniz Rumores** original-piano edition, the next **14-work balanced acquisition queue** should contain Clementi Op.36 No.1, Kuhlau Op.20 No.1, Beethoven Op.49 Nos.1–2, Scarlatti K.11, Debussy Préludes I Nos.1–3, Chopin Op.6 No.1, Mendelssohn Op.30 No.6, Liszt S.172 No.3, Satie Gnossiennes Nos.2–3 and Joplin *Bethena*. This proposed mix supplies four Classical, one Baroque, three Impressionist, three Romantic and three early modern works without adding another block of Bach studies. Selection is contingent on rights, completeness and current-catalogue checks, not a promise that all fourteen are ready. If accepted after those gates, each raises the distinct-work count once regardless of asset or movement count.

Spanish follow-up first resolves Tango’s Hugo Ries edition, then acquires exact original-piano Granada, Sevilla and Asturias files without counting guitar arrangements or Asturias aliases as new compositions. The other highest-value follow-up research is narrower: identify a clearly reusable early Mozart edition for a needed Köchel number, complete Haydn movement bundles, obtain Boulanger's actual solo-piano MXL/PDF files, and resolve Ravel/Granados/Fauré territorial and edition evidence. This creates a repeatable composer → work → exact edition pipeline, while period balance guides selection rather than substituting for provenance.
